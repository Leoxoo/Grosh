# People in With are records, not typed names

A transaction's With holds **Persons**: stored records with a name, an optional phone, a hidden flag and a last-used date, shared by every transaction that names them. Before this, With was one typed string per transaction, so "Pasha" on two transactions was two unrelated strings, there was nothing to hang a phone, a contact link, an "Owes me" total or a Show by people list on, and a Loan could not be with two people. A Person is a many-to-many relationship with Transaction, so a dinner can be with several people while a Loan or Debt is held to exactly one by the transaction rules (not by the store, per ADR-0001). People and places are the same thing: a restaurant is a Person too.

Names are the Person's identity, compared ignoring case: typing "pasha" reuses "Pasha". Nothing is `.unique` (ADR-0001); the one place that adds a Person (`Person.add(named:phone:in:)`, or `PeopleByName` for code that adds many) looks the name up first. Hiding a Person (`Person.hide()`) only sets a flag: they leave the With menu's Previous section but keep their name and their place on every transaction, and typing their name again clears the flag. Choosing a Person moves their last-used date, which orders Previous.

`Transaction.withName` stays as a stored string, now a copy of the people's names joined with `; `, kept in step by `Transaction.setPeople(_:usedAt:)`, the one place that sets a transaction's people. It exists because lists, search, the CSV export and Debts & Loans rows read the With for many transactions at once, and walking the `people` relationship on each would fault it from the store once per row (docs/agents/architecture.md). Anything that changes a Person's name must refresh the copy on their transactions. Keeping the old attribute rather than dropping it is also what lets SwiftData open an existing store with a lightweight migration: adding the `Person` model and the `people` relationship is purely additive, and the old names survive for `Person.migrateWithNames(in:)`, which `GroshStore.makeSeededContainer` runs when the store opens. It turns every distinct name (ignoring case) into one Person, spelled as the newest transaction spells it, and gives every transaction, those in Recently Deleted too, its Person. It does nothing once the store has any Person.

A Person may later be linked to an entry in the user's Contacts. `Person.contactIdentifier` is the optional link and `phone` the number; both exist from the start so adding Contacts is not a second schema change. The link is only an identifier: Grosh never copies a contact's other details, and a Person with no link stays an ordinary name.
