# Expected reimbursements are records beside the Expense; a payback into another wallet is a linked transaction

Two decisions about money that people owe the user for an Expense.

**An Expected reimbursement is a record of its own** (`ExpectedReimbursement`: a Person, an optional amount, a reminder day, and Forgiven and Closed marks), kept beside its Expense and deleted, restored and purged with it. It is not a Reimbursement and not a transaction, so it counts in no balance, list or report and nothing has to be added to or taken from any of them when one is made, edited, removed or forgiven. Its amount is optional: a blank amount is an open amount, left out of totals and flagged. We rejected storing it on the Expense (several people, each with an amount, a reminder and a state would have needed a list of structured values inside `Transaction`) and storing it as a Reimbursement with a "not yet" flag (every balance, net and report reads Reimbursements, so each would have had to learn to skip those). A Person is a relationship on it, which is what Show by people reads (`ExpectedReimbursements.byPerson`). Forgiving follows ADR-0004: the record is marked, nothing is added, and since it never counted anywhere, nothing changes. Its reminder reuses the Loan, Debt and Estimated-amount reminder plumbing (`PendingReminder`, `ReminderSource`, the Home bell), under its own notification identifier prefix and category (`grosh.owes-me`), so an action such as Text them can be added to the notification without touching the others. Where the status is worked out (open, paid back, closed, forgiven) is one place, `ExpectedReimbursement.status`, from the Reimbursements and paybacks recorded against it; nothing about it is stored twice.

**Money paid back into the Expense's own wallet stays a Reimbursement on the Expense** (ADR-0008), recorded against the expectation. **Money paid back into another wallet is a real transaction there**, dated the day it was received, filed under a new locked Reimbursement category (an Income category, never offered in a picker), with the Person in With, and linked to the Expense by `linkID` like the halves of a Transfer (ADR-0002), so Related transactions and Recently Deleted already treat them together: deleting the Expense offers to delete the payback too, and they come back together. This supersedes the last sentence of ADR-0008 ("A Reimbursement paid into a different wallet is not a Reimbursement; the user records it as a transfer or income"). Because it is a transaction in its own wallet, that wallet's balance, list and report count it as money in, and the Expense's own wallet keeps the full amount, with a note of what was reimbursed to which wallet. In the Total it behaves like a Transfer half: the Total list hides it, and the Expense shows its net there, `Transaction.totalViewNetCents`, its net less what was paid into wallets counted in the Total. A payback into a wallet left out of the Total is not in the Total, so the Expense keeps that part, and a payback whose Expense is outside the Total or in Recently Deleted stays in the Total as the money in it is. The Total report and the Month graph count the Expense by that number and the payback as nothing; a single wallet's report counts the Expense by its wallet-level net and the payback as Income under Reimbursement (`Transaction.countedAmount(in:otherIncome:)`). Balances never read the Total-view number: they read each wallet's own transactions, so the Total balance, the Total list and the Total report agree.

The cost is that the Total-view net depends on which wallets are in the Total, and a stored figure would go stale whenever a wallet's "Include in total" changes, a payback is deleted or restored, or a wallet is archived. `totalViewNetCents` therefore reads the Expense's paybacks when it is asked, which walks relationships, but only for an Expense with `hasPaybacksElsewhere`, a stored flag set when its first payback is recorded and never cleared, so the 16k store's other transactions never leave the stored values (docs/agents/architecture.md). The flag may read true for an Expense whose paybacks are gone, which costs a look and nothing else. A second cost is that an Expense with paybacks shares its link with them, so it cannot also be a part of a Split (ADR-0007): a part of a Split cannot be paid back into another wallet, and an Expense paid back into another wallet cannot be split. The payback itself is edited only by deleting it (it has no editor of its own, like a Debt Collection), and the Expense stays in its wallet while paybacks into other wallets exist.
